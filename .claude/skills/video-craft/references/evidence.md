# Deliver the promise first, then earn every cut

The best-supported rule in creator video is not about cutting at all. **The opening must visibly pay off the title and thumbnail**, within the first frame of a Short and the first 5–30 seconds of a long-form video. YouTube's own retention tooling ties first-30-second retention to that match, MrBeast's internal memo blames mismatches for click-offs, and at least five named strategists say the same thing independently. The second strongest finding reverses popular belief. **The pace at which new information and problems arrive matters more than how often the editor cuts.** MrBeast publicly abandoned "overstim" editing in 2024, and his sampled cut rate fell from 38 to 23 cuts per minute. Ex-MrBeast strategist Mario Joos, Paddy Galloway and scriptwriter George Blackman all say story density, not cut speed, drives retention. The hardest numbers an AI editor can apply come from adjacent industries with published standards: **−14 LUFS / −1 dBTP** delivery loudness, **20 characters per second** and **42 characters per line** for on-screen text (Netflix), **90% title safe** (SMPTE), Material and IBM Carbon easing curves, and the **1/(2×fps)** shutter rule. Many of the most-repeated creator numbers are weak or false. These include "cut every 2–3 seconds," "aim for 70% retention," the 8-second goldfish attention span, and exact pixel safe zones for organic posts. The agent should therefore be strict about physics and perception, and empirical about taste, using each video's own retention graph to settle questions of taste.

*Confidence labels used below. **High** means a published standard, official platform documentation, or three or more independent credible sources agree. **Medium** means two sources agree, or there is one strong primary source such as the MrBeast memo or a creator's own transcript. **Low** means a single vendor blog, an anecdote, or an AI summary. **Derived** means arithmetic or synthesis from cited figures, not a number any source states.*

## The first 30 seconds are measured, and the measurement rewards a literal payoff

Platforms measure the opening explicitly, and the opening is where viewers leave fastest. YouTube's retention report has a dedicated **"Intro" metric: the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds**. YouTube says a high value suggests the opening matched the title and thumbnail. It also defines "Top moments" as segments where almost no one dropped off, and recommends moving such content earlier because audience size shrinks over time ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)). That gives the agent an official basis for "move the strongest moment forward." MrBeast's leaked 2024 production memo makes the same point in plainer terms. "The first minute of each video is the most important minute," and in one example **21M of 60M viewers left within it**. For minutes 1–3 the instruction is "stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them" ([memo text](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/)). The memo's authenticity rests on a former employee's leak, confirmed by four others; MrBeast's PR declined to comment ([Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/?p=81151)).

Paddy Galloway gives the most concrete long-form intro template. **"Confirm the click"** with something that matches the thumbnail, establish context by about **7 seconds**, and introduce a **new, unexpected hook by about 30 seconds**, plus a promise that staying pays off ([Colin and Samir transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)). His cited example is Ryan Trahan recreating his thumbnail as the opening shot ([Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1508549799242522633.html)). The Colin and Samir hosts give the same 7-second figure ([playbook transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)). Galloway appears often on that show, so the two sources are not fully independent. For short-form, the best quantified evidence is a TikTok x CreatorIQ study of *ads*, not organic posts, with no sample size reported. Showing a person in the first couple of seconds raised "hooking power" by **50%**, and saying "you" in the first 5 seconds was associated with **+128% purchase intent** ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok)). Jenny Hoyos says in her own words that "the hook needs to be very visual." She follows it with a foreshadow of **3 seconds or less** that "just needs to have some sort of expectation," and her top Shorts average **"exactly 34 seconds"** ([Creator Science #167](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). Mark Rober frames the same idea as emotion before explanation: "I can't teach you if I don't have your attention" ([Rober transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/mark-robers-60-million-dollar-experiment)).

The structure after the hook has one primary blueprint. The MrBeast memo calls for "crazy progression" through minute 3. It then specifies a **re-engagement at ~3:00**, "simple" high-novelty content with quick scene changes from 3:00 to 6:00, and a **second re-engagement at ~6:00** that pushes the story forward. In the back half it says "don't ever signal the end," and "endings must always be abrupt to protect retention." The memo's reference length is an average of **818 s (~13:37)** over the last 100 videos ([memo text](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). For videos far from 13 minutes, scale those timestamps proportionally (derived). The connective tissue is the Parker and Stone rule: beats join with "but" or "therefore," never "and then" ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/04/tony-zhou-storytelling-video-essay-f-fake-therefore-but-and-then)). Blackman's segment rule adds that "every segment is like a mini video in itself," with its own hook, stakes and resolution ([Blackman](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-5-why-should-i-care)). The Try Guys' version is simply "once they see the big reveal at the end, the video is over" ([playbook transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)). Shorts loops now pay off mechanically. **Since March 31, 2025, every Shorts start or replay counts as a public view**, while monetization still uses "engaged views" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)). Hoyos's loop is a concrete model: open on a blurred room and close on the same shot unblurred, so the rewatch "gives them new context" ([PickScribe summary](https://pickscribe.com/v/nXckk5vAk2w)). Platform signals differ by surface. On Reels, Mosseri reportedly named **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach** as the top signals ([Torro](https://torro.io/blog/3-most-important-instagram-metrics-for-reach)). On Instagram, reposted or lightly watermarked content loses recommendation eligibility to the original ([PetaPixel 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)). Cross-posts should therefore be re-cut and stripped of watermarks rather than uploaded as copies.

## Information density, not cut density, sets the pace

The most important pacing finding is a public reversal. On 3 March 2024 MrBeast wrote that he had "slowed down our videos, focused on story telling, let scenes breathe." He called for an end to "the ultra fast paced/overstim era of content… It doesn't even work." Tubefilter counted **38 cuts per minute** in a March 2023 video and **23 cuts per minute** in a 2024 video. That is roughly one cut every 1.6 s versus one every 2.6 s (derived). Tubefilter warns that this rests on one sampled minute per video and that the view gains cannot be attributed to the change ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)). Mario Joos goes further: "If you are looking at fast cuts, then you are looking in the wrong place." In his view, slowness comes from stacking non-progressive scenes, and the real audit is for visual, auditory, character and structural variety ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/insiders/mario-joos-youtube-retention-secrets/)). Blackman says pacing is "pace of new problems," not pace of editing ([playbook transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-youtube-playbook-in-37-minutes)). Galloway says "the best retention hack is better ideas" and warns against "a knitting channel with the pace of MrBeast" ([Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1508549799242522633.html)). He also notes that MKBHD's Autofocus cuts "very minimally—like two cuts" ([transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)). For the agent, a pattern interrupt is valid only when it carries new information. A b-roll shot that shows what is being said, a number graphic, or a location change all qualify; a cut that fires on a timer does not. The "Dips" marker in YouTube's retention report is the authoritative guide to where interrupts are needed ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)).

Short-form remains faster, but its numbers are soft. Vendor guides recommend a jump cut, zoom or SFX every **2–4 seconds** ([ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/tiktok-hook-strategies/)). One claims that a scene change every 2–3 s "correlates with 82% retention" across 5,000 Shorts, but shows no method ([Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-shorts-retention-tips-2026)). Chanmama's in-house Douyin analysis reports **2.7 shot changes in the first 3 s** for viral videos versus 1.2 for ordinary ones; this figure is unverified ([蝉妈妈](https://www.chanmama.com/yunyingquan/article/1176.html)). Hoyos wants "a pacing break without the pacing actually breaking" ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). A defensible default is a meaningful visual or information change every 2–4 s in Shorts, with rewatch and loop design doing more work than cut count. In film, average shot length fell from about **12 s (1930) to about 2.5 s** and has plateaued since around 2000 ([Stephen Follows](https://stephenfollows.com/many-shots-average-movie/); [Widescreen Journal](https://widescreenjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/formatted-cutting-rates.pdf)). No reliable ASL exists for YouTube tutorials, documentaries or TikTok.

Within a cut, the classical grammar still governs invisible editing. Murch's Rule of Six weights **emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye-trace 7%, 2D plane 5%, 3D space 4%**. Criteria are sacrificed from the bottom up, so a continuity-perfect cut that drops an emotional beat is the wrong cut ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch)). Murch himself called the numbers "a little bit facetious," so treat them as priorities, not arithmetic ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-books/)). The 180-degree rule, the 30-degree rule and cutting on action are standard continuity tools ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-continuity-editing-in-film/)). For eye-trace, place the incoming shot's point of interest where the viewer's eye already sits at the end of the outgoing shot ([Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/eye-trace-and-rule-of-six-editing/)).

Talking-head jump cuts need specific hygiene. Alternate framing with a **10–15% punch-in** on one side of the cut, so the scale change reads as a new shot rather than missing frames ([Jupitrr](https://jupitrr.com/how-to/edit-talking-head-videos); [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/easy-ways-to-hide-jump-cuts-your-next-video); [Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/what-are-jump-cuts/)). Punch only at sentence starts, and never at every cut, because constant zooms make the head "twitch" and draw complaints ([Vizard](https://agent.vizard.ai/how-to/stop-the-punch-ins-cycling-too-fast-in-a-talking-head-edit.html)). Hide the remaining cuts under b-roll, screen recordings or reaction shots, or on a blink or nod ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/easy-ways-to-hide-jump-cuts-your-next-video)). Silence removal should keep a pad, not cut to zero. The open-source auto-editor defaults to a **0.2 s margin** around speech ([auto-editor docs](https://auto-editor.com/ref/options)). Practitioners warn that gaps under about **150–200 ms** produce a "machine-gun" rhythm ([claudskills](https://claudskills.com/skills/video-editing-pro/SKILL.md)). Interview editors keep breaths and pauses that "help convey the context of the thought" ([Production Expert](https://www.pro-tools-expert.com/production-expert-1/2020/7/29/editing-audio-interviews-what-choices-do-we-make-and-why)). Do not cut every beat: "the most drama is in those silences," and a held look can replace a page of dialogue ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2017/05/08/edit-with-silence/)).

J- and L-cuts are well defined but have no standard length. A J-cut starts the incoming audio before its picture; an L-cut carries the outgoing audio under the new picture ([Wikipedia: J cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_cut); [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/how-to-edit-videos-l-cuts-and-j-cuts/)). The only concrete figure found is a vendor's **6–12 frames at 30 fps (0.2–0.4 s)** for a quick dialogue line ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/j-cuts-and-l-cuts-dialogue-edits)). Dialogue tends to use more J-cuts than L-cuts, because a sound makes us turn to look ([Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/j-cut-film-editing-how-to-75535/)). Long J-cuts of ambient sound bridge scenes, as the waves in *Saving Private Ryan* do ([Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/j-cut-vs-l-cut/)). When a new speaker enters, do not run a long unidentified off-screen voice ([Pippit](https://www.pippit.ai/blog/j-and-l-cuts-for-dialogue)).

## Sound follows hard numbers: −14 LUFS, a 12–20 dB duck, and fades on every edit

Audio is the area of the playbook with the most published specification, but YouTube's target is measured, not published. YouTube normalizes playback to about **−14 LUFS** and only turns loud content *down*. That figure comes from third-party measurement, and MeterPlugs warns against mastering everything blindly to −14 ([Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-loudness/); [MeterPlugs](https://www.meterplugs.com/blog/2019/09/18/youtube-changes-loudness-reference-to-14-lufs.html)). Spotify officially normalizes to −14 LUFS ([Spotify for Artists](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). Broadcast standards are firm: EBU R128 is **−23 LUFS ±0.5 LU with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP**, and ATSC A/85 is **−24 LKFS with true peak ≤ −2 dBTP** ([iZotope](https://s3.amazonaws.com/izotopedownloads/docs/insight200/en/loudness/index.html)). Apple Podcasts asks for −16 LUFS. TikTok and Instagram publish no target; about −14 is observed for TikTok ([Fora Soft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026)).

Mix craft is less codified. A University of Salford paper notes that aesthetic ducking practice "is not defined by mixing handbooks and broadcaster recommendations" ([Salford](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1489990)). The working figures are a **−20 dB "typical" duck**, with −6 to −12 dB as a gentle dip ([Vindral](https://composer-docs.vindral.com/operators/operators-sidechain-ducking.html)), and Premiere auto-ducking examples at −12 dB (subtle) and −18 dB (noticeable) ([Magnific](https://www.magnific.com/blog/how-to-use-auto-ducking-in-premiere-pro-for-clean-professional-audio)). Every audio edit needs a short fade or crossfade, because cuts at loud instants click and cuts between different room sounds bump. Edge fades of **5–50 ms** are the practical start, over a continuous room-tone bed ([Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/audio-how-to/audio-editing/how-to-hide-audio-transitions-for-a-cleaner-sounding-video/); [Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/blog/crossfade-audio/)). Premiere's minimum audio transition is **2 frames**. One editor uses 2 frames on dialogue and 10 on SFX ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/why-is-the-minimum-audio-transition-duration-2-frames/m-p/12864872)). For transition SFX, sound-library vendors agree that a whoosh should **start before the cut and peak on the first frame of the new shot**, with its tail trimmed and an optional hit at the landing ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide); [Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/cinematic-whoosh-sound-effects/)).

Capture sets the ceiling. Put a lav **6–8 in (15–20 cm) from the mouth**, pointed at it ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/audio/how-use-lavalier-microphones-interview-video-production-168250); [Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/article/c4/1193-sound-reasoning-lavalier-lessons/)). Record **30–60 s of room tone** at the same position and settings ([Syracuse Newhouse](https://nhtraining.syr.edu/field-sound-recording/)). Set dialogue levels in the **−12 to −6 dBFS** zone ([Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/alc/mastering-audio-levels/); [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-set-the-perfect-audio-levels-for-video)). 32-bit float recording protects the capture, but exports can still clip ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254032566)).

## Graphics that replace the picture should run on subtitle-grade timing

The full-screen infographic is the one interrupt that always carries new information. It also has no creator-specific spec, so the most defensible timing rules come from subtitle standards and UI motion systems. Kurzgesagt states the core principle for VO-driven animation: the narrator's voiceover "provides the timing for the animation team" ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/)). Applied to an NLE, the voice track stays untouched on A1 while the graphic covers V2. The cutaway starts and ends on narration sentence boundaries, and each data element appears as the voice names it. Netflix gives the measurable limits. Adult reading speed is capped at **20 characters per second**. The **minimum event duration is 20 frames**. Gaps between consecutive text events are **either exactly 2 frames or at least 0.5 s**. In-times fall on the first frame of the audio, or within 1–2 frames of it. If speech starts within 0.5 s after a shot change, the text snaps to the cut ([Netflix timing guidelines](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Timing-Guidelines); [Netflix style guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)). BBC/Ofcom guidance is **160–180 WPM**, and above 200 WPM is hard for many viewers ([Clevercast](https://clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines)). Practitioners independently converge on "read it twice" as the hold rule for on-screen text ([SSW Rules](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules-legacy/post-production-do-you-give-enough-time-to-read-texts-in-your-videos/); [Creative COW](https://creativecow.net/?p=928622)). Text should stop moving before the reading period begins ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/text-animation-for-readability-kinetic-type-without-overcomplication)). Combining these gives a derived hold formula: **hold = max(0.8 s, characters ÷ 10 s) plus the entrance animation**, capped by how long the narrator stays on the point. A 30-character label therefore holds about 3 s, which fits the practitioner ranges of **1.5–3 s for short labels and 3–6 s for lower thirds** ([Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/how-long-should-on-screen-text-stay-youtube)).

Easing and duration have hard published references. Material Design 3 uses **emphasized-decelerate `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)` for entrances** and **emphasized-accelerate `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15)` for exits**. Its duration tokens run from short (50–200 ms) through medium (250–400 ms) and long (450–600 ms) to extra-long (700–1000 ms) ([material-web tokens](https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)). IBM Carbon sets 70 ms for micro-interactions, 240 ms for expansions and 400 ms for large expansions. It says duration should scale non-linearly with distance and size, and that expressive motion runs slower than productive motion ([Carbon Motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). At 30 fps, a 250–400 ms entrance is 8–12 frames (derived). Overshoot in an After Effects-class graph editor comes from dragging the incoming Bezier handle past the target value so the curve settles back ([Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/the-bounce-and-overshoot-animation-trick-every-motion-designer-should-know)).

Kinetic creator captions are a convention, not a standard, and all specs come from caption-tool vendors. The consensus is **2–4 words on screen**, a heavy all-caps sans such as Montserrat Black or Anton, and the active word popped with a slight bounce and highlighted in yellow (#FDE047 or #F7C204) or green ([Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-captions); [Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/tools/recreate-video-editing-style/alex-hormozi/)). Japanese telop practice is the most codified regional style. It calls for about **16 characters per line**, gothic fonts, an outline plus a subtle shadow, **at most 5–6 caption designs**, and a sound effect on emphasis captions ([kk-arale](https://video-editing.kk-arale.com/youtube-caption-rules/)), at about 6–8% of frame height ([Adobe Japan](https://www.adobe.com/jp/creativecloud/roc/blog/video/telop.html)). For conventional captions, Netflix caps lines at **42 characters and 2 lines**. It prefers bottom-heavy breaks and never splits article+noun, first+last name or verb+auxiliary ([Netflix style guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)).

Placement is where sources conflict most. 16:9 is settled: **Title Safe is 90% and Action Safe is 93%** (SMPTE ST 2046-1) ([Netflix Backlot](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406208331923-Title-Safe-and-Safe-Action-Best-Practices)). For 9:16 at 1080×1920, every official spec is ad-focused. Meta's Reels ad spec reserves the **top 269 px (14%), bottom 672 px (35%) and 65 px on each side** ([Bundle.social](https://bundle.social/tools/safe-zone-checker)). AdaptlyPost's conservative organic estimates are TikTok top 108 / bottom 320 / right 120, Reels top 210 / bottom 310, and Shorts top 120 / bottom 300 ([AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide)). Reap explicitly calls such figures "working margins, not official universal specifications" ([Reap](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones)). Taking the worst case of AdaptlyPost's figures gives a derived union box of **x 60–960, y 210–1600**. Ads, or a stricter policy, should use Meta's tighter band. Contrast follows WCAG: **4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text** ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Over moving video that ratio holds only with a stroke, shadow or semi-opaque plate. For delivery, render overlays as **ProRes 4444 with alpha**, and make the alpha interpretation (straight or premultiplied) identical in compositor and editor, or edges fringe ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/straight-vs-premultiplied-alpha-on-import/m-p/12453563/highlight/true)).

## Camera fundamentals set a ceiling that the edit cannot raise

Most shooting rules are textbook-stable, and their value to an editing agent is diagnostic: knowing what can be rescued. For framing, put the eyes on the top third line, leave modest headroom, and leave look room on the side the subject faces ([LibreTexts](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/City_College_of_San_Francisco/Video_Production_Handbook_I/08%3A_Video_Aesthetics/8.05%3A_Video_Composition); [Ilex](https://www.ilexinstant.com/headroom-lead-room-video)). For motion, the 180° shutter rule gives **24 fps → 1/48 (1/50), 30 → 1/60, 60 → 1/120** ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-the-180-degree-shutter-rule/)). In 50 Hz regions, 25/50 fps at 1/50 avoids flicker ([Camera Jabber](https://camerajabber.com/?p=62469)), and critics note the rule only roughly holds between 24 and 60 fps ([ProVideo Coalition](https://provideocoalition.com/the-180-shutter-angle-rule-is-broken)). For lighting, place a soft key about **45° off-axis, slightly above eye level**, often a window, with fill on the shadow side and a back light for separation ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/3-point-lighting-youtube-guide)). Never mix daylight and tungsten on a face; gel one source to match the other, or use daylight-balanced LEDs ([Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/article/c13/8933-light-source-the-color-of-light); [4K Shooters](https://4kshooters.net/2021/04/24/working-with-mixed-color-temperature-light)). On exposure, Canon suggests **zebras at 70 IRE** for skin in Rec.709 ([Canon Asia](https://snapshot.asia.canon/en/article/videography-faq-false-colours-waveform-and-zebras)). Practitioners put light skin nearer **55–60 IRE** and darker skin lower ([DVinfo](https://www.dvinfo.net/forum/open-dv-discussion/341200-zebras-70-what.html); [Wolfcrow](https://wolfcrow.com/an-overview-of-the-tools-available-for-exposure-in-the-atomos-shogun/)). Log footage reads low until converted: ~42% on a Log-C waveform equals ~55–60% in Rec.709 ([Datum](https://datum-website.pages.dev/blog/exposure-tools-that-lie)). On iPhone, AE/AF lock ties exposure and focus together ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256085851)). Cinematic-mode focus can be re-set afterwards in Final Cut Pro ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver1fd04bc0e/mac)).

The color order of operations is consistent across sources. **Correct first, then match shots, then apply the look.** Neutralize white balance and saturation before touching skin, and use the vectorscope skin line at about 11 o'clock as a hue reference, not a hard target, because forcing skin onto the line can turn it green ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-vectorscope-definition/); [DaVinci Resolve Club](https://davinciresolveclub.com/skin-tone-correction-davinci-resolve/); [Noam Kroll](https://noamkroll.com/the-best-order-of-operations-for-color-grading-why-it-makes-all-the-difference/)). Reframing has a pixel budget. A 4K clip in a 1080p timeline is only at full resolution up to **200% scale** ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256204508)). A 9:16 crop from a 3840×2160 frame is about 1215×2160 (derived), which leaves little extra punch-in room. Stabilization and punch-in crops multiply, so a 10% stabilization crop plus a 1.5× punch-in totals about 1.65× (derived). Warp Stabilizer's "Crop Less ↔ Smooth More" slider trades smoothness for scale. Rolling-shutter reduction requires the Subspace Warp or Perspective method ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/warp-stabilizer-settings.html)). For b-roll, build the shot list line by line from the script ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/how-to-shoot-broll-youtube); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/fr/blog/post/create-b-roll-youtube-videos/)). One guide separates a **2.5–4 s edit duration from a 7–12 s capture roll** so each action has handles ([ChatCut](https://chatcut.io/blog/how-to-shoot-b-roll)). Inserts must relate to the spoken line ([Captions.ai](https://captions.ai/blog/practical-guide-b-roll-video)).

## Myths, conflicts and gaps the agent must not paper over

Several of the most repeated numbers in creator culture fail on inspection. The agent should never cite them as justification. Every decision should rest on platform metrics and the video's own retention curve.

The real conflicts fall into four groups. The deepest is pace. A 2021 school of thought cuts every pause over half a second and claims jump cuts flow at "a dozen cuts per minute" ([Digital Machiavelli](https://digitalmachiavelli.substack.com/p/digital-machiavelli-2-video-editing)). Korean variety style uses **SFX every 3–5 s** and heavy reaction captions ([koreaexperience](https://koreaexperience.com/blog/korean-youtube-culture-popular-creators-and-trends)). Against both stand MrBeast's 2024 reversal and Japan's explicit telop restraint ([kk-arale](https://video-editing.kk-arale.com/youtube-caption-rules/)). The resolution that fits all the evidence is genre. Where captions and SFX *are* the comedy, density works; in explainers and story-driven long-form, it reads as noise. The second group is measurement: key-to-fill ratios (1.3:1–2:1 vs 2:1–3:1), skin IRE (70 vs 55–60), 9:16 caption Y-position (1200–1550 vs 250–1410 vs "never the bottom third") ([Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-placement-short-form-video); [KreateFlo](https://kreateflo.com/blog/where-to-place-captions-in-916-video-for-tiktok-and-reels)), and whether white balance or exposure comes first in correction. The third is creator self-contradiction. Hoyos says retention is why Shorts take off but also that "retention doesn't matter as much as people think," and her one-second-trim figures differ between her interview (83%→88%) and a Medium write-up (70%→45% dip) ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); [Medium](https://medium.com/@facurubinn/heres-how-an-18-year-old-youtuber-amassed-10-million-views-using-youtube-shorts-848f190b5b8b)). Joos warns that over-optimizing the first 30 seconds stalls creators even as every platform metric centers on it ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/insiders/mario-joos-youtube-retention-secrets/)). The fourth is b-roll ratio. Vendors claim **60/40 A:B**, **40–60% coverage** and **50–70% for short-form** with no data, and no measured ratio exists for any named channel ([Overseer OS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/how-to-use-b-roll-in-youtube-videos); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-add-b-roll-to-talking-head-video)).

The gaps are large and specific. Reddit and Threads were unreachable, so the forum consensus on punch-in frequency, music level and b-roll hold time is **unverified, not absent**. No platform has published A/B data comparing hook types for organic content. TikTok and Instagram publish no loudness or completion benchmarks. No source gives a standard J/L-cut length, ducking attack/release times, or a frame tolerance for cutting on the beat. Primary material from Film Booth, Think Media, Johnny Harris, Casey Neistat, Hormozi and Dhruv Rathee was not retrieved, so the playbooks attributed to them rest on summaries. Korean-language, Hindi-language and Latin American creator sources are missing, and Chinese guidance is mostly 2022-era operator blogs. On the technical side, the research found no per-platform frame-rate guidance and no current iPhone Cinematic-mode limits. It also lacks sourced HDR/HLG-to-SDR pitfalls, LUT opacity practice, After Effects expression references, and a minimum mobile type size.

## Conclusion

The evidence hierarchy runs opposite to the folklore. The numbers creators repeat most (cut cadence, attention spans, retention targets) are the least supported. The most reliable numbers were written for other industries: subtitle reading speeds, broadcast loudness, SMPTE safe areas and UI easing curves. An AI editor should therefore work in two modes. It should apply **hard constraints** without negotiation: loudness, reading speed, safe areas, contrast, shutter, continuity axis, the promise-payoff check, and fades on every audio edit. It should treat **taste parameters** (cut rate, punch-in scale, J-cut length, duck depth, caption style) as tunable defaults checked against the video's own Dips, Spikes and Intro metric after publication.

The deeper implication is that the full-screen infographic, often treated as decoration, is the interrupt that best fits what elite practitioners now say drives retention. A graphic delivers new information on the narrator's clock, so it raises "pace of new problems" without adding the arbitrary cuts that MrBeast's team abandoned. The most valuable automated checks are therefore semantic, not rhythmic. Does frame 1 show the thumbnail's object? Does each beat join with "but" or "therefore"? Does every cut, zoom or graphic introduce something the viewer did not already know? Does the video end within seconds of its final reveal?
